How Audio Oscillators Work

An audio oscillator generates repetitive, alternating electronic waveforms. In the digital realm using the Web Audio API's OscillatorNode, these waves are computed mathematically at thousands of samples per second:

  • Sine Wave: Contains only the pure fundamental frequency with zero harmonic overtones. Ideal for acoustic calibration and tuning.
  • Square Wave: Rich in odd-numbered harmonics, creating a bright, hollow electronic tone common in early digital synthesizers.
  • Sawtooth Wave: Contains both odd and even harmonics, producing a sharp, buzzy timbre used to synthesize brass and string soundscapes.
  • Triangle Wave: Contains odd harmonics that drop off rapidly, offering a warmer tone than a square wave.

Understanding Tempo Measurement

Beats Per Minute (BPM) measures the speed of a piece of music by quantifying the number of rhythmic beats occurring within a 60-second window.

This BPM tool tracks the precise time intervals between sequential taps, calculating a rolling mathematical average to eliminate micro-timing errors and yield a stable tempo value.

Wave Physics

Harmonic Overtones & Timbre

Timbre describes the unique acoustic quality of a sound that allows human ears to distinguish between different instruments playing at the same pitch. While a pure sine wave consists of a single frequency, complex sounds contain a fundamental frequency combined with overtones and harmonics.

Digital Signal Processing

The Nyquist-Shannon Theorem

To accurately reconstruct a continuous analog audio signal digitally without introducing aliasing distortion, the sampling rate must exceed twice the highest frequency component present in the original audio signal. Standard CD audio uses a 44.1 kHz sample rate to capture up to 20 kHz human hearing limit.

Acoustics

Decibel Levels & Loudness

The decibel (dB) scale is a logarithmic unit used to express the ratio of acoustic pressure relative to the threshold of human hearing. Because human loudness perception is nonlinear, an increase of 10 dB represents approximately a doubling of perceived loudness.

Web Audio API

Real-Time Web Audio Context

Modern browsers handle real-time digital signal processing through the Web Audio API using modular audio nodes. Audio sources connect to gain controls, filters, and analyzers before outputting to the sound card.
